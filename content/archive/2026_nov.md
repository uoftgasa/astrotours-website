abstract: "Photons from distant celestial objects encode much more than is visible to the eye. Exoplanets, planets beyond our Solar System, pose a unique technical challenge to experimental astronomers for their characterization. In this talk, I will present some of the creative solutions derived by astronomers for observing these new worlds and their environments. Astronomers have learned to squeeze every last bit of information from photons emitted by planetary systems, and I hope to share my fascination for the current astronomical instruments that sit at the technological forefront of exoplanet science."
speakerInfo: "Emily is a Ph.D. student in the Department of Astronomy & Astrophysics at the University of Toronto. She builds novel space instruments in the ultraviolet, visible, and infrared wavelengths for characterizing exoplanets. Her work focusses on bringing cutting-edge technologies to astronomy, and quickly maturing these instruments to be flown and tested in space. Outside of astronomy, Emily enjoys exploring her new home Toronto, learning new dance forms, and staying active outdoors."
